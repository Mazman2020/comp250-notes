# Note 3: News Coverage Does Not Mean Fast Attacks

Quotation
---------

“Our data has continued to show that exploit release and media attention are not
predictive of exploitation timelines. While in some cases these data points are
correlated, the trends do not currently show that these factors should dictate
prioritization or constitute an elevated response to a given vulnerability” (Charrier
and Weiner 2024, under “Implications”).

Commentary
---------

This pushes back on our argument. We say that once a flaws description is public, AI
can build an attack in hours. But Mandiant found that a public exploit does not always
lead to a fast attack. Of the 2023 flaws that got news coverage and had a public exploit
72 percent were not known to be attacked (Charrier and Weiner 2024). Mandiant
says what matters more is how hard a flaw is to use and how valuable it is to attackers
(Charrier and Weiner 2024, under “Implications”). My answer is that AI makes flaws
easier to use. PHP2Shell was also very valuable since PHP runs a huge part of the web.
So our claim should focus on big flaws like that, not every flaw.

Subject terms
---------

exploit availability; time-to-exploit; prioritization; disclosure
