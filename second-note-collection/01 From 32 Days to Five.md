# Note 1: From 32 Days to Five

Quotation
--------

“Then, across all of 2021 and 2022, the average observed TTE [time-to-exploit]
dropped further to 32 days, already half of our first tracked TTE starting in 2018. In 2023,
we observed the largest drop in TTE thus far, with an average of just five days” (Charrier
and Weiner 2024, under “Time-to-Exploit”).

Commentary
--------

Our storyboard uses this number for our first reason so I looked at what it
actually measures. Mandiant counts attacks that happen “before or after a patch is
released” (Charrier and Weiner 2024, under “Time-to-Exploit”). That means the average
includes zero days which are flaws attacked before any fix exists. In 2023, zero days made
up 70 percent of the flaws Mandiant tracked (Charrier and Weiner 2024, under “Zero-Day
vs. N-day Exploitation”). So five days is not exactly the time companies have to patch. This
still helps our argument in one way which is the big drop happened before AI hacking tools were
common  so AI is shrinking a window that was already short.

Subject terms
--------

time-to-exploit; patch window; measurement; zero-days
